## What is a day rate, and why does it matter?

The day rate is what a freelancer bills for one day of work. It generally covers:

-   **Fixed costs** (contributions, licences, equipment).
-   **Non-billable time** (prospecting, staying current, admin).
-   **The margin** needed to invest and afford time off.

For a quick calculation: target annual income ÷ billable days (around 180 in France) = floor day rate. Add 20% for negotiation margin, and you have your "list price" day rate.

## FAQ: everything about day rates

### 1\. How do I convert a monthly salary into a day rate?

Take your target gross annual salary, add 45% to cover social contributions, time off and non-billable time. Divide by 220 (working days), then by 0.8 to keep a 20% safety buffer. Example: €48,000 gross → 48,000 × 1.45 / 220 / 0.8 = a **€395** day rate. Adjust for the rarity of your skill and the client context.

### 2\. Can a day rate be negotiated down for a long mission?

Yes — most freelancers grant a 10–15% discount from 60 days onwards. The argument: schedule visibility and less time spent prospecting. On the client side, offer a firm commitment (a minimum number of days) rather than a sliding "maybe": the independent will absorb the discount much more easily.

### 3\. Which tools help track a day rate's profitability?

Apps like Toggl, Harvest or [Freebe.io](http://Freebe.io) track time and invoices. Paired with Notion or Google Sheets, they show the real margin per project. The goal: keep your "effective" average day rate at ≥ 90% of your list rate — otherwise it's time to raise your prices or streamline your processes!
