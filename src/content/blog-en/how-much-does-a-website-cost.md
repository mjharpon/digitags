## What does designing a website involve?

Designing a website spans several disciplines and skills. It involves creating a pleasant and functional user interface, as well as organising the content to meet visitors' needs. This process isn't limited to aesthetics alone; it also includes technical aspects such as [**user experience**](/en/roles/freelance-ux-designer) (UX), [**search engine optimisation**](/en/roles/freelance-seo-consultant) (SEO) and responsiveness across different devices. Ultimately, designing a website means making sure it's both beautiful and high-performing.

Good website design starts with understanding the business's objectives and users' expectations. This often requires in-depth research into the target audience, market trends and the competition. The aim is to create a platform that not only attracts visitors but also encourages them to engage with the content and take the desired actions, such as buying a product or signing up for a newsletter. The design must therefore be user-centred, taking their needs and behaviours into account.

Then, building a website also extends to the development phase, where the mock-ups and prototypes come to life. There are two options from there: either code, using programming languages such as HTML, CSS and JavaScript to build the site's features, or no-code or low-code. In short, designing a website is an art that combines creativity and technique.

## **The costs involved in designing a website**

When it comes to designing a website, it's important to understand that there are a multitude of costs involved in the process. There are, of course, the design fees, which include the work of designers and developers. These costs can vary considerably depending on experience and your needs — but we'll come to that right after. A freelance designer may charge less than an agency, which can be a good option if you want to optimise the cost/quality ratio.

Next, there are the costs of the tools and software needed for the design. Content management systems (CMS) such as WordPress, Webflow or Shopify may have subscription fees or costs associated with themes and plugins. It's essential to factor these expenses into the total design budget. Subscriptions are generally in the region of €30-50 per month, and if you run an e-commerce site, you need to allow for commissions on sales.

That leaves hosting and domain name costs. A domain name is your site's web address, and it must be registered for a set period, usually one year. You can expect to pay €10-20 per year for the domain name. Web hosting is needed to make the site accessible on the internet. These elements, although they may seem secondary, are the foundations for your site to work properly and must therefore be included in your initial budget.

## **The factors that influence the cost of a website**

Several factors can influence the final cost of designing a website. One of the main ones is the complexity of the project. A simple showcase site, with a few pages and basic features, will generally cost less than a full e-commerce site with integrated payment systems, product catalogues and advanced features. The more complex the project, the more time and resources it will require, which will increase the total cost.

Then comes the level of customisation expected. A bespoke design that perfectly reflects your brand identity will take more effort than a standard template used by lots of businesses. Customisation often involves additional research and design iterations, which can push prices up.

The technology used for the design can also have a significant impact on costs. For example, a 100% coded site will always be more expensive than its low-code or no-code equivalent.

# **How much does a website cost?**

Estimating the cost of designing a website for your business requires you to clearly define your objectives. Is it a simple showcase site, an e-commerce platform or a blog? Here are a few price ranges according to the main categories of needs:

-   A landing page (a single page) will cost you between €400 and €2,000
-   A basic showcase site with a few pages will cost you between €1,000 and €5,000
-   An e-commerce site will cost you between €3,000 and €10,000
-   A platform site or something more complex will cost you between €5,000 and €10,000
