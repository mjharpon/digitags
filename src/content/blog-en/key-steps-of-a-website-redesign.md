## **Why consider a website redesign?**

With advances in technology and changes in user behaviour, a site that was once perfectly good can quickly become outdated. Visitors' expectations change: they're looking for smoother user experiences, and modern designs and content. By ignoring these changes inherent to digital evolution, you risk losing potential customers to competitors with an up-to-date site.

A redesign lets you fix any problems with your current site, such as technical issues (slow loading times, broken links or complicated navigation). These issues can not only frustrate users but also hurt your search engine rankings. By reworking them, you improve not only the user experience but also your online visibility, which is essential for attracting and retaining your audience.

A redesign is also a good opportunity to reassess your brand and make sure your website properly reflects your [**visual identity**](/en/projects/visual-identity). Trends evolve, so you need to make sure your online presence is in step with the image you want to convey.

## **Step 1: audit the current state of your website**

Before launching into a redesign, you can start by auditing the current state of your site. Begin by analysing technical performance: loading speed, mobile compatibility and accessibility. Use analytics tools to measure all of this.

Next, examine your site's content. Is it up to date and relevant to your target audience? Identify the pages that generate traffic and those that don't seem to be working. This will help you determine what needs to be improved or removed during the redesign. Also keep an eye on your existing SEO: are your keywords still relevant? Do you have untapped content opportunities that could attract more visitors?

The gold standard, though, is to run surveys or customer/user interviews to understand what they like or dislike about your current site. Their feedback can provide valuable insights to help steer the redesign. By incorporating your users' perspectives, you make sure your new site will meet their expectations even better.

## **Step 2: define your objectives and target audience**

Do you want to increase traffic, improve your conversion rate or boost your brand awareness? With precise objectives, you'll be able to guide every decision related to the redesign, from design to content creation. It will also let you measure the success of your project once it's launched.

In parallel, identify your target audience. Who are your ideal customers? What are their expectations, behaviours and preferences? Create personas that represent your audience to better understand their needs. The clearer your picture of your audience, the better placed you'll be to create content and a design that win them over (because this is clearly a charm offensive).

To help you, feedback from your marketing, sales or customer service teams should be taken into account.

## **Step 3: review your website's structure**

Draw up a sitemap listing all the important pages and their hierarchy. This will help you visualise the structure and make sure every page has a logical place. A good sitemap improves not only the user experience but also SEO.

Avoid creating too many pages, group similar information together and create clear categories. The aim is for visitors to quickly find what they need. Also think about adding internal links to direct users to other parts of your site, which can help strengthen your SEO.

Whether it's signing up for a newsletter, requesting a quote or buying a product, include easy-to-reach CTAs. Make sure your site structure supports your objectives by placing these elements in the right spots.

Optimising your content for SEO is the next step: work relevant keywords into your content to improve your visibility on search engines. Start by carrying out keyword research to identify those relevant to your sector and target audience. ## **Step 5: design and layout of the new website**

Your site shouldn't just look good — it also needs to work well. Make sure the elements of your site are well arranged and that the visual hierarchy guides users towards the important information.

When it comes to layout, favour a readable structure. Use white space to give the content room to breathe and avoid information overload. Users need to be able to scan information quickly, so don't hesitate to use lists, subheadings and short paragraphs.

## **Step 6: test the new version of the website**

Before publishing your new website, it's essential to run a few tests to check that everything works properly. You need to check all links, forms and features. Use testing tools to simulate different usage scenarios and identify problems before going live. Involve real users in the testing process to get feedback on the user experience and spot elements that could be improved further.

Make sure the design and features remain consistent, whether on Chrome, Firefox, Safari or mobile devices. Testing across several platforms ensures that all your users will enjoy an identical, optimal experience, however they access your site.

Once the tests are done and the adjustments made, it's time to shine! Time to sign off the final version of your site.

## **Step 7: content migration and redirects**

Once you've signed off your new site, the next step is to migrate the content from the old site to the new one. It's a delicate step that must be handled with care so as not to lose valuable information, and to preserve your SEO. Start by drawing up a complete list of all pages and their content, then prepare a migration plan.

Set up 301 redirects for all old URLs that have been changed or removed. This sends users and search engines to the new pages without losing traffic. Redirects also help preserve your domain authority and avoid 404 errors, which can frustrate visitors and hurt your SEO.

Once everything is in place, do a final check to make sure the migration went smoothly and your site is up and running.
