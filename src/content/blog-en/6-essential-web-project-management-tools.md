Juggling tight deadlines, scattered files and group chats heading off in every direction? Take a deep breath: we've got what you need.

Here are **10 essential tools** to manage your web projects like a pro – without ending up curled in a ball under your desk. Whether you're a [**designer**](/en/roles/freelance-ux-designer), a [**developer**](/en/roles/freelance-web-developer), a [**project manager**](/en/roles/freelance-project-manager) or a bit of everything at once (hang in there), these tools will save you time, energy… and maybe a few hairs.

## **Why does good web project management change everything?**

On the web, everything moves fast. Very fast. And between client requests that change every two hours, deadlines that are sometimes too short and resources that are always too limited, rock-solid project management is key. Otherwise, it's an open door to bugs, sleepless nights and deliverables that are "almost" finished.

Good project management means:

-   Clear tasks, assigned and tracked.
-   Timing under control (or at least closely monitored).
-   Resources used intelligently.
-   And smooth communication.

Managing a web project well means increasing your chances of delivering something high-quality, on time, without sacrificing your mental health.

## **The superpowers of web project management tools**

Why do these tools deserve a place in your toolbox?

-   **Top-notch organisation**: No more stray Post-its or files called "todo\_final\_v3\_bis\_revised\_ok.pdf". Everything is centralised, tidy and easy to find.
-   **Simpler communication**: No more sending 36 emails to find out where the project stands. Pick the right platform and everyone is up to date in real time.
-   **The big picture**: With sexy dashboards (yes, they exist), you know exactly where you are, who's doing what and what's left to do.
-   **Fewer surprises**: Tracking progress = avoiding bad news at the last minute.

## **The best tools to manage your tasks**

### Trello, Asana, ClickUp & co

These tools are a bit like diaries 3.0: you create tasks, assign them, set deadlines and see it all in a nice board (Kanban or list view, depending on your preference).

Ideal for knowing who's doing what (and when), for ticking boxes and for collaborating without stepping on each other's toes.

Bonus: you can comment, attach files and like updates – in short, turn your task management into a productive social network.

## **Tools to manage your time**

### Clockify, Harvest, Toggl

These time-tracking tools are there to answer THE awkward question: "So how many hours did we actually spend on this?"

They let you track precisely who's doing what and how long it takes, and draw useful conclusions to plan better in future (or to invoice with a smile).

Some even go as far as offering ultra-detailed reports – perfect for [project managers](/en/roles/freelance-project-manager) who love beautiful charts and Excel spreadsheets.

## **Project tracking tools**

### Jira, [Monday.com](http://Monday.com), Notion

Now we're entering the heavyweights' club. These tools are made for people who like to have **the big picture** without losing sight of the details.

Dashboards, performance indicators, deadlines, dependencies between tasks: everything is there to steer your project like a captain.

And for fans of agile or [**Scrum**](/en/blog/scrum-vs-v-model): good news, these tools are often tailor-made for you.

## **Tools to store your files**

### Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive

With these platforms, everyone gets the right files, in the right version, at the right time. You can work on documents simultaneously, add comments or restore a previous version (when someone has deleted everything "by accident").

In short, you avoid lost files, dubious duplicates and meltdowns at 11pm the night before a delivery.

## **Team comms tools**

### Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord

Let's not kid ourselves: communication is often what makes or breaks a project.

These tools let you chat in real time, share files and create channels by project, by topic, by team (or just for the jokes, let's be honest).

And bonus: they integrate with your project management tools to notify you when a task changes status. Enough to stay up to date without juggling 10 tabs.

## **Quality management tools**

### TestRail, Selenium, Postman

These tools let you check that everything works properly before pushing it all to production. You can plan tests, track results, spot flaws… and sleep a little more soundly at night.

Add security tools such as OWASP ZAP or Snyk, and you've got a real safety belt for your web projects.

## **How do you choose the right tools?**

No need to take them all. The right combination depends on:

-   The size of your team
-   The nature of your projects
-   Your personal preferences (interface, integrations, budget…)

Test, tinker, involve your team and see what genuinely makes your life easier.

And remember: **a good tool is one you actually use**.
