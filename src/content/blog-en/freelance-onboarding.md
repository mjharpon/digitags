## Step 1 – Before D-0: prepare the ground

### 1.1 Write a SMART brief

Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound. No need for a PowerPoint fit for a strategy committee: a clear, concise Google Doc is enough, as long as it meets the 5 criteria.

### 1.2 Gather the tooling

-   Slack/Teams access
-   Shared drive (Google, Notion, SharePoint…)
-   Templates

### 1.3 Appoint an internal "buddy"

56% of talent are big fans of the buddy system. This person will become the lighthouse in your freelancer's digital night: quick Q&As, translating in-house jargon, deadline reminders when everyone's running on fumes.

### 1.4 Send a carefully prepared welcome pack

Example for a [**freelance UI designer**](/en/roles/freelance-ui-designer): full brand guidelines + Figma access.

Example for a [**freelance SEO consultant**](/en/roles/freelance-seo-consultant): editorial guidelines, keyword list, key contacts.

## Step 2 – D-0 to D-1: connect, align and build trust

### 2.1 Kick-off meeting (30 min, strictly timed)

1.  **Project pitch** in 5 slides (objectives, KPIs, audiences).
2.  Potential blockers > mitigation plan.
3.  Sign-off on the backward schedule.

### 2.2 Set the communication rules

-   Main channel + reporting frequency.
-   Feedback method (start/stop/continue or 4×4×4, your call).
-   Emergency process (who gets pinged for a production bug at 10 pm?).

### 2.3 Align business and personal expectations

A happy freelancer delivers 2× faster. Clarify: working hours, time zones, preferred deliverable formats.

## Step 3 – D-1 to D-2: get started and secure follow-up

### 3.1 First test delivery

A mini-deliverable or a draft lets you validate the format without waiting for the final deadline.

### 3.2 Feedback sandwich

Positive → areas for improvement → positive. It motivates and corrects gently.

### 3.3 Shared dashboard

Trello, Notion, Asana… The dashboard becomes the shared compass: tasks, due dates, % progress.

### 3.4 Senior management check-in

Give your freelancer visibility: a quick hello on video with the marketing director is often enough to multiply their commitment tenfold.

## 5 classic mistakes to avoid when onboarding a freelancer

1.  **Confusing onboarding with training**: your goal isn't to turn the freelancer into a stand-in for a permanent contract (CDI) hire, but to make them operational on your specific challenge.
2.  **Drowning them in paperwork**: contracts, NDAs, brand guides—fine. The 382-page quality manual? Thanks, but no thanks.
3.  **Forgetting company culture**: even remotely, rituals and values matter. A simple virtual coffee chat sometimes works wonders.
4.  **Skipping the test phase**: course-correct on a mini-deliverable rather than a major release.
5.  **Ignoring continuous upskilling**: a freelancer evolves, and so do your needs. Plan regular check-ins to realign ambition & budget.

## FAQ

### How should you structure a freelance onboarding checklist?

Group your actions by timing: _Before D-0_ (access, contracts), _D-0_ (kick-off, tools), _D-1_ (test deliverable) and _D-2_ (feedback, sign-off). A visual checklist in Notion or Trello does the job.

### What information should go in a welcome pack?

Tool access, key contacts, brand guidelines, SEO/UX guidelines, calendar and roadmap, and—if possible—a short welcome note.

### Should you always appoint a buddy for a freelancer?

90% of the time, yes: the buddy cuts response times and humanises the relationship. Keep it for engagements lasting more than a week.

### How long should freelance onboarding take?

For standard engagements, 48 hours is enough provided you've prepared the ground. Beyond a week, the opportunity cost climbs.

### Which tools make it easier to integrate a freelancer?

Notion for docs, Slack for comms, Loom for asynchronous feedback, Toggl for time tracking and Yousign for signing without scanning.
