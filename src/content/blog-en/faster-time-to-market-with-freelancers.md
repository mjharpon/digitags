For a CMO or a business leader, the conclusion is crystal clear: it's no longer "do it fast _or_ do it well", it's **do it fast _and_ do it well**. ## Classic pitfalls and how to avoid them

The first pitfall is the "catch-all" brief. Wanting to transform everything at once dilutes energy. It's better to choose a tangible business objective: loading speed, basket conversion, customer service response time, feature adoption, and build measurable deliverables around it. It's simpler to estimate, steer and celebrate.

Second pitfall: the tool jungle. Twelve solutions to communicate, document, track and design means just as much cumulative friction. Standardise. One chat, one living doc, one shared backlog, one design system. The rest is secondary, especially if you're aiming to **staff quickly** and ramp up at lightning speed.

Third pitfall: security treated as an afterthought. NDAs, access rules, Git policy, anonymisation of sensitive data… all of this must be ready before the **freelancer** arrives. It's reassuring, it's professional and, above all, it avoids interrupting the acceleration curve a week after kick-off.

## How much does it really cost?

Yes, a senior **freelancer**'s day rate can seem higher than an equivalent salary. But the real bill isn't limited to the day rate. The full cost of a permanent contract (CDI) includes social charges, onboarding, managerial oversight, the learning curve and leave, not to mention the risk of a mismatch. Over a rolling twelve months, many teams find that a well-framed **freelance engagement** works out 20 to 30% cheaper for greater impact and faster deployment. Add a 15% margin to the budget to absorb the unexpected, and compare with a clear head. Most of the time, the balance tips towards the independent when **time-to-market** is the critical variable, but not only then 😉

## FAQ

### How do you calculate the budget for **bringing in a freelancer**?

Start by estimating the days needed per deliverable: user research, design system, front-end integration, analytics set-up, CRM migration… Add them up and include a 15% margin for the unexpected. Then compare with the **full cost** of a permanent contract (social charges, onboarding, management, leave, hiring lead time). Over a twelve-month horizon, a well-framed **freelance** set-up often works out 20 to 30% cheaper, while delivering faster.

### Is the **freelance** model suitable for sensitive projects (data, compliance)?

Yes, provided it's framed seriously. A systematic NDA, limited access rights, separate environments (staging/production), code review and logging of sensitive actions. At **digitags.work**, this foundation is standard. Many of our talents already operate in regulated contexts (PSD2, GDPR, ISO) and know how to document without slowing down.

### Which KPIs should you track to measure the impact on **time-to-market**?

Three indicators are enough to start with: the actual **Idea → MVP** duration and the **adoption lead time** (activation, retention, revenue) of the feature delivered.
