Looking to design a high-performing digital product, but not sure where to start? Here are the key stages to follow.

From research to design, through development and testing, you'll see how to identify your users' needs, define an effective strategy, design an intuitive user interface and run thorough tests — then keep improving your product continuously.

## **The key stages of designing a digital product**

### **Understanding users' needs**

To design a high-performing digital product, understanding users' needs is essential. The first stage is in-depth user research to identify who your target users are and what they expect. You can use methods such as interviews, surveys or observation to gather valuable information about their needs, preferences and behaviours.

Once you've gathered that data, you can analyse it to identify the main problems your product can solve. That lets you clearly define your product's goals and make sure it answers your users' real needs.

Understanding users' needs is the single most important point — and the absolute starting point — for designing a high-performing digital product. By factoring in their expectations from the start, you raise the odds of building a product that delights them.

### **Defining the product's goals**

Before you start designing your digital product, it's essential to clearly define the goals you want to reach. They can relate to user satisfaction, new customer acquisition, revenue generation or any other KPI of your choosing.

To define your goals, ask yourself: What value does your product add for your users? What results do you want it to deliver? How will you measure its success?

Once your goals are defined, use them as a guide throughout the design process. They'll steer your choices and keep you focused on what really matters to your product's success.

### **Designing the user experience (UX)**

User experience (UX) design is one of the most important stages — and arguably the most interesting — in building a high-performing digital product. UX covers every interaction between the user and the product, and aims to create a smooth, intuitive, enjoyable experience.

To design an effective user experience, you must factor in your users' needs, expectations and behaviours. The main goal of UX design is to make using the product as simple and pleasant as possible for the user.

### **Creating a beautiful user interface (UI)**

An attractive user interface (UI) is the finishing touch of a high-performing digital product. UI covers the product's visual appearance, including the layout, colours, fonts and icons used.

To create a pleasing UI, apply design principles such as visual hierarchy, consistency, readability and usability. The interface should be both aesthetically pleasing and functional. At digitags, many talented [**freelance UI designers**](/en/roles/freelance-ui-designer) can support you on this.

When designing the UI, also factor in the technical constraints and the requirements of your development platform, so your product stays compatible with the devices and browsers your users rely on.

### **Developing the product with an agile team**

Developing a digital product takes close collaboration between the designers, the [**web developers**](/en/roles/freelance-web-developer) and the [**product owner**](/en/roles/freelance-product-owner) or [**product manager**](/en/roles/freelance-product-manager). An agile approach eases that collaboration and enables iterative, incremental product development.

An agile team is defined by its ability to adapt quickly to change, communicate effectively and work collaboratively. Short development cycles make it possible to gather user feedback quickly and fold it into the product.

Agile methodologies such as Scrum or Kanban help organise the development team's work and keep high-value features shipping regularly. They also promote transparency and accountability within the team. Feel free to call on one of our [**freelance agile coaches**](/en/roles/freelance-agile-coach) for support.

### **Testing and improving the product**

Testing and improvement are essential stages to guarantee your digital product's quality and performance. Tests run at several levels: functional tests, usability tests and performance tests.

Functional tests verify that every product feature works as intended. Usability tests evaluate how easy the product is to use and identify any ergonomic issues. Performance tests make sure the product behaves optimally, even under heavy load.

Once testing is done, gather users' feedback and the data, and fold them into a continuous improvement process. That can lead to design adjustments, feature updates or other improvements based on users' needs and feedback.

### **Launching and marketing the product**

Now it's time to take your product to market. You'll need a clear launch and marketing strategy.

The launch strategy can include a website, marketing content, advertising campaigns and strategic partnerships. Also define sales and tracking goals to measure how effective your go-to-market strategy is.
