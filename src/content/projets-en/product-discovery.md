Looking to launch a new product? The product discovery process is essential to understand customers' needs, explore market opportunities and build a product that will meet consumers' expectations.

Product discovery is one of the key stages of your [**product strategy**](/en/projects/product-strategy). Here's why it matters.

## All about product discovery

## Why run a product discovery?

A carefully run product discovery brings many benefits. By understanding customers' needs, exploring market opportunities and building a product that meets consumers' expectations, you maximise your chances of success. Here are some of the main benefits:

1.  Deep understanding of customer needs : product discovery gives you detailed knowledge of your potential customers' needs and wants, so you can build a product that answers their expectations and fully satisfies them.
2.  Exploration of market opportunities : explore the market and identify the opportunities open to you. You can spot emerging trends, unmet needs and gaps in the existing offer.
3.  A high-performing product : by understanding customers' needs and exploring the market's opportunities, you can build a distinctive product that stands out from the competition.
4.  Lower risk : running a product discovery lets you validate your ideas with your target audience before the official launch. That reduces the risk of failure and lets you adjust your product based on customer feedback.
5.  Better odds of success : by following a structured product discovery approach, you make decisions grounded in data.

## **The key stages of product discovery**

A successful product discovery involves several key stages. For all of them, we recommend bringing in an expert and surrounding yourself with a good product team. If you don't have one in-house, meet our [**freelance product owners**](/en/roles/freelance-product-owner) and [**product managers**](/en/roles/freelance-product-manager). Here are the main stages:

### **Identifying the project's needs and goals**

The first stage of product discovery is identifying the project's needs and goals. You must clearly define what you want to achieve with your product, which problem you want to solve and which objectives you want to reach. This stage steers your research and development efforts.

### **Research and data collection**

Once the project's needs and goals are identified, run in-depth research to gather data. That includes a market study, competitive analysis, a look at industry trends and data on customers' behaviours and preferences. The more data you collect, the better your chances of building a great product.

### **Analysing and synthesising the collected data**

Once the data is collected, analyse and synthesise it to extract insights. This stage reveals customers' needs, market trends and the opportunities open to you. Data analysis techniques and visualisation tools make this stage easier.

### **Generating ideas and concepts**

Based on the insights drawn from the analysis, you can start generating ideas and concepts for your product. A creative, out-of-the-box mindset matters here to develop novel concepts. Brainstorming techniques, [**design thinking**](/en/projects/design-thinking) workshops and visualisation tools all help stimulate creativity.

### **Prototyping and iterations**

Once you have promising concepts on paper, you can move to prototyping. Prototyping lets you create early versions of your product to test and iterate quickly. Rapid prototyping tools — paper mock-ups, virtual prototypes or 3D models — bring your ideas to life. You can also bring in a [**freelance UX designer**](/en/roles/freelance-ux-designer) to help if you're building a digital product.

### **Testing and validating the product**

Once you have a working prototype, put it in front of your target audience and validate it. That gathers precious feedback and tells you whether your product genuinely meets customers' expectations. Usability tests, surveys or interviews provide additional insights.
