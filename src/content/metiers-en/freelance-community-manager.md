## What is a freelance community manager?

A freelance community manager is an independent professional who manages social networks and online communities on behalf of their clients. As a freelancer, they work autonomously and offer their services to one or several clients at the same time. Their main role is to create and maintain a social media presence for brands and companies, interacting with users and driving engagement.

The freelance community manager brings skills in communication, marketing and, of course, social media management. They are responsible for creating and managing content (photos, videos, carousels, stories…), joining and answering users' comments and messages, engaging the account with other content, and analysing the performance of their work.

## **Essential tools for a freelance community manager**

The freelance community manager has a whole range of tools at hand. Here are some of the essentials:

1.  Publishing schedulers : Schedulers such as Hootsuite, Buffer or Sprout Social let you plan and publish content across platforms — saving time and keeping your content organised.
2.  Social media analytics tools : Use social analytics tools — including the ones just mentioned, which also provide analytics — to track the performance of your efforts, with detailed statistics on engagement, impressions, clicks and more.
3.  Content management tools : Tools such as Trello, Asana or Basecamp help organise tasks and collaborate with clients: dashboards, task assignment, progress tracking and communication with the team.
4.  Content creation tools : Use creation tools such as Canva to design visuals for your social media posts.

These are just a few examples of a freelance community manager's essential tools — the right picks depend on needs and budget.

## **The skills a community manager needs**

Excelling as a community manager takes several must-have skills. First, an excellent command of the different social networks (no surprise there). The community manager must understand each platform's specifics — Facebook, Instagram, X, YouTube or LinkedIn — and adapt the content accordingly. That knowledge is what maximises engagement and reaches a well-targeted audience.

Writing skills are essential too. The community manager must write clear, concise, engaging messages — able to tell a story, spark emotions and drive action.

Analytical skills are also required: the community manager must read performance data and adjust the strategy. In the end, a good community manager combines creativity, communication skills and analytical ability.

## **How to build and manage an online community**

Building and managing an online community takes a well-defined strategy. Start by clearly defining the community's values and mission — that attracts members who share the same interests and sets a framework that encourages exchanges. The community manager also identifies the target personas to better understand their needs and expectations.

Once the community is established, it's time to bring it to life: publishing content regularly, asking questions and encouraging conversations. The community manager must also be proactive in managing interactions, answering comments and asking people for their input.

## **The challenges and opportunities of the community manager's craft**

One of the main challenges is staying relevant while publishing consistently. Social platforms regularly change their algorithms, and trends move fast — the community manager must stay on constant watch to adapt.

Another challenge is managing time and resources. With so many tasks, from content creation to performance analysis, the community manager must prioritise and use the right tools to make the most of their time.

On the flip side, the community manager's role is increasingly recognised as essential to companies' communication strategy — and demand for these professionals keeps growing.
