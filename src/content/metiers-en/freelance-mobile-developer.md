A freelance mobile developer designs, builds and maintains iOS and Android apps. Whether native with Swift and Kotlin or cross-platform with React Native and Flutter, they turn a user journey into a fast, stable app that's live on the App Store and Google Play.

Launching an MVP, rebuilding an ageing app, shipping a key feature or taking over a codebase nobody wants to touch: an experienced mobile developer joins your product team for the length of the mission and ships an app your users can rely on.

## What a freelance mobile developer does

A mobile app isn't just a smaller website. Mobile developers deal with constraints specific to smartphones: patchy connectivity, battery life, permissions, push notifications, countless screen sizes and the review cycles set by Apple and Google.

Their work spans the whole chain: app architecture, implementing the designs, consuming the APIs built by the [**back-end developer**](/en/roles/freelance-back-end-developer), testing, setting up the build pipeline and publishing to the stores. Once live, they track quality through crash reports and user feedback.

Day to day, they work alongside the product owner, designers and back-end team, and can make the case for a technical choice: native or cross-platform, offline support, state management, update strategy.

## Typical mobile development missions

Depending on how mature your product is, the mission can look very different:

- **Mobile MVP**: design and ship a first publishable version within weeks to test real usage with real users.
- **Rebuild or migration**: move from UIKit to SwiftUI or from XML views to Jetpack Compose, or rewrite an ageing app without breaking what works.
- **New feature**: in-app purchases, notifications, geolocation, offline mode, biometric sign-in or a third-party SDK integration.
- **Performance and stability**: cut startup time, memory usage and crash rate, usually starting from Firebase Crashlytics or Sentry data.
- **Release engineering**: set up mobile CI/CD (Fastlane, Bitrise, GitHub Actions), code signing, test builds and staged rollouts.

## Native or cross-platform: the tech they master

Our freelance mobile developers cover both main approaches. Native means Swift and SwiftUI on iOS, Kotlin and Jetpack Compose on Android — the right call for apps that push the hardware, rely on rich animations or need the latest OS APIs on day one.

Cross-platform, React Native (often with Expo) and Flutter let a single team share most of the code between iOS and Android. Kotlin Multiplatform is also gaining ground for sharing business logic while keeping native UIs.

Beyond the language, a good mobile developer knows their way around App Store Connect, Google Play Console and TestFlight, the review guidelines of both stores, and the privacy requirements that come with mobile under GDPR: consent, privacy labels and data safety forms, and how analytics and ad SDKs are handled.

## When to bring in a freelance mobile developer

A few situations make a strong case for adding mobile expertise to your team:

- **You're launching an app**: the need is validated and you want a solid first version without building a whole team.
- **Your app is ageing**: falling store ratings, recurring crashes, technical debt that slows every release.
- **Your team is web-first**: your developers know front-end inside out, but not the specifics of mobile platforms and stores.
- **You have a hard deadline**: a commercial launch, a compliance update or a new iOS or Android release to absorb.

## How a mobile development mission unfolds

Mobile missions run iteratively, with testable builds in your hands early on:

### Technical scoping

The developer reviews the need, the designs and any existing code, then recommends the approach (native or cross-platform), the architecture and the tooling. They flag the risks upfront: dependencies, missing APIs, store constraints.

### Iterative delivery

Features ship in short sprints, with builds distributed through TestFlight and Google Play testing tracks. The product owner and pilot users test on real devices as the work progresses.

### Testing and quality

Unit tests, UI tests, code review and accessibility checks come with every delivery. Crash reporting is wired in before anything reaches production.

### Release and follow-up

The developer prepares store submissions, handles review feedback and rolls out progressively. They then monitor stability metrics and document the code so your team can pick it up smoothly.

## Mobile, front-end or full-stack developer: what's the difference?

A [**front-end developer**](/en/roles/freelance-front-end-developer) builds web interfaces that run in a browser; a mobile developer ships apps installed on the phone, subject to store rules and review cycles. A React developer can move to React Native, but publishing, on-device performance and native APIs remain a craft of their own.

A [**full-stack developer**](/en/roles/freelance-full-stack-developer) covers both the front and back end of a web application.
