A freelance data engineer lays the foundations of your data strategy: pulling data from your tools and applications, centralising it in a warehouse or lakehouse, transforming it and making sure it lands on time, complete and reliable. Without one, dashboards and models are built on sand.

Working with a freelance data engineer means getting the architecture right from the start, migrating an ageing stack, or freeing up a data team that spends more time fixing pipelines than delivering value.

## **What a freelance data engineer does**

Data engineers design, build and maintain the chain that takes data from its source to the people and systems that use it. They choose ingestion tools, model tables, write transformations, orchestrate jobs and set up the tests and alerts that flag a problem before it reaches a dashboard.

At heart, it's a software engineering role: version-controlled code, reviews, CI/CD, infrastructure as code. Data engineers work daily with data analysts and data scientists — their first users — and with the product and tech teams that generate the data.

## **Typical missions**

These are the projects our clients most often bring a freelance data engineer in for:

- **Setting up a modern data stack**: choosing the warehouse, ingestion tool and transformation layer, and shipping the first production pipelines.
- **Migration**: moving from a legacy ETL or an on-premise warehouse to Snowflake, BigQuery or Databricks without breaking reporting.
- **Ingestion pipelines**: connectors to your CRM, marketing tools, ERP or application databases, in batch or real time.
- **Quality and governance**: data tests, observability, a data catalogue, access management and handling of personal data.
- **Optimisation**: cutting processing times and the cloud bill, reworking data models that have become unmanageable.

## **The tools our freelance data engineers use**

Our data engineers master the building blocks of today's data architectures:

- **Languages**: SQL and Python every day, Scala or Java for some Spark workloads, Bash for tooling.
- **Storage and compute**: Snowflake, BigQuery, Databricks, Redshift, and open table formats such as Apache Iceberg and Delta Lake.
- **Ingestion and transformation**: Fivetran, Airbyte, dbt, Spark, Kafka for streaming.
- **Orchestration and quality**: Airflow, Dagster, dbt tests, Great Expectations, data observability tools.
- **Infrastructure**: Terraform, Docker, CI/CD pipelines on GitHub or GitLab, managed services on AWS, GCP or Azure.

## **When should you bring in a data engineer?**

The warning signs are easy to spot: numbers don't match from one dashboard to the next, manual exports keep piling up, a pipeline breaks every week, or your [**data scientist**](/en/roles/freelance-data-scientist) spends most of their time cleaning data. It's also the right moment when you launch a new offer and want to measure it properly from day one.

A data engineer is different from a [**data analyst**](/en/roles/freelance-data-analyst), who uses data to answer business questions, and from a [**DevOps engineer**](/en/roles/freelance-devops-engineer), who looks after application infrastructure more broadly. In smaller teams, an analytics engineer often bridges data engineering and analysis, with dbt as the central tool.

## **How a data engineering mission runs**

A well-run mission delivers value quickly, then consolidates.

### Audit of the existing setup

The data engineer maps your sources, flows, tools and use cases, and pinpoints weak spots, duplicates and unnecessary costs.

### Target architecture

They propose an architecture that fits your data volumes, in-house skills and budget — rather than whatever stack is trending. Every choice is documented and agreed with you.

### Incremental build

Pipelines are delivered one business domain at a time, starting with the one that has the most impact. Each delivery is tested, versioned and monitored.

### Handover

Documentation, coding conventions, runbooks and knowledge-transfer sessions: your teams must be able to evolve the platform without depending on the freelancer.

## **Security and GDPR built into the architecture**

A data platform, by definition, concentrates sensitive data. A freelance data engineer applies least-privilege access, separates environments, masks or pseudonymises personal data and enforces the retention periods GDPR requires. They also keep an eye on data residency and processing traceability — topics your legal and security teams will be glad to find already documented.
