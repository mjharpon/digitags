A freelance data analyst makes your data speak. They define the right metrics, build the dashboards your teams actually open, and answer the questions your decisions hinge on: why conversion is dropping, which channel pays off most, which features keep users coming back.

Working with a freelance data analyst gets you reliable answers fast, brings order to scattered reporting, or builds a data culture in a team that still steers by gut feeling.

## **What a freelance data analyst does**

The data analyst is the bridge between data and business teams. They understand what's at stake for product, marketing or finance, turn their questions into queries, sanity-check the numbers and present their findings in a clear, actionable way.

The job goes well beyond producing charts. A good data analyst challenges existing metrics, spots misleading interpretations, documents definitions so everyone means the same thing by "active user", and recommends concrete actions based on the analysis.

## **Typical missions**

These are the needs our clients most often bring a freelance data analyst in for:

- **Reporting and dashboards**: designing KPIs, building team-level dashboards, automating manual reports.
- **Product analytics**: funnels, cohorts, retention and feature adoption, supporting the product team's decisions.
- **Marketing performance**: ROI by channel, attribution, campaign tracking alongside the traffic manager.
- **Tracking plan**: defining the events to track, testing the implementation, aligning it with user consent.
- **One-off analyses**: market sizing from internal data, pricing analysis, diagnosing a drop in activity.

Our data analysts adapt to your stack and master the most widely used tools:

- **Querying**: advanced SQL on BigQuery, Snowflake, Redshift or PostgreSQL, and dbt to make analysis models dependable.
- **Visualisation and BI**: Looker, Power BI, Tableau, Looker Studio, Metabase.

- **Advanced analysis**: Python (pandas, notebooks) or R for statistical analysis, segmentation and A/B tests.

## **Data analyst, data scientist or data engineer?**

A data analyst explains what happened and why, to inform decisions. A [**data scientist**](/en/roles/freelance-data-scientist) builds models that predict or automate. A [**data engineer**](/en/roles/freelance-data-engineer) supplies both with clean, reliable data.

For most companies, the data analyst is the first data profile to bring in: they create value quickly from the data you already have, and surface the needs that will later justify a data engineer or data scientist. They work closely with the [**product manager**](/en/roles/freelance-product-manager), feeding directly into prioritisation.

## **How a data analysis mission runs**

Every mission follows a structured path, from business question to decision.

### Gathering needs

The data analyst meets the teams involved to understand the decisions ahead, the open questions and the metrics already in use.

### Defining the metrics

They formalise the KPIs, their precise definitions and their sources. This shared reference puts an end to debates about the numbers in meetings.

### Analysis and readout

Queries, consistency checks, visualisations: results are presented with their limits and concrete recommendations — not just charts.

### Team autonomy

Dashboards are documented and your teams trained to use them, so they can answer everyday questions on their own.

## **What you get at the end of the mission**

Depending on the scope, a data analysis mission delivers documented, operational dashboards, a metrics dictionary shared by every team, a validated, GDPR-compliant tracking plan, and written analyses with prioritised recommendations. These deliverables feed straight into your [**product discovery**](/en/projects/product-discovery) work and your budget decisions.
