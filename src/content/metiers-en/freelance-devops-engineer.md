A freelance DevOps engineer bridges the gap between development and operations. They automate how code is tested, shipped and run, so your teams can release more often, with less risk and without late-night firefighting.

CI/CD pipelines, containers, infrastructure as code, monitoring, security and cloud cost control: an experienced DevOps engineer lays solid foundations, then hands your team what it needs to keep them running.

## What a freelance DevOps engineer does

DevOps is first and foremost a culture: bringing the people who write the code closer to the people who run it. A freelance DevOps engineer turns that culture into concrete tools and practices. They design the pipelines that test and deploy every change, describe infrastructure as version-controlled code, and set up the monitoring that raises the alarm before your users notice anything.

They're just as useful on a young product, where the goal is to keep things simple and robust, as on an established platform that has become hard to evolve: manual deployments, hand-configured servers, drifting environments, cloud bills spiralling out of control.

Depending on the context, you'll also hear about SREs (Site Reliability Engineers) and platform engineers: the former focus on reliability in production, the latter on the internal tooling that makes developers' lives easier.

## Typical DevOps missions

The missions our freelance DevOps engineers take on fall into a few main families:

- **CI/CD**: design or harden GitHub Actions or GitLab CI pipelines, from automated tests to continuous deployment, with on-demand preview environments.
- **Containers and orchestration**: containerise applications with Docker, then run them on Kubernetes (EKS, GKE, AKS) — or on simpler managed services when that's enough.
- **Infrastructure as code**: describe your cloud with Terraform or OpenTofu, structure the modules and make every environment reproducible.
- **Cloud migration**: move an application to AWS, Google Cloud or Azure, or between providers, while keeping downtime to a minimum.
- **Observability**: logs, metrics and traces with Prometheus, Grafana, OpenTelemetry or Datadog, alerts that matter and shared dashboards.
- **FinOps**: break down the cloud bill, right-size resources, automatically shut down idle environments and make costs visible per team.

## Security and compliance built in

Security is part and parcel of DevOps work. Secrets live in a dedicated vault rather than in the code, cloud access follows the principle of least privilege, dependencies and container images are scanned in the CI, backups are actually tested and the recovery plan is written down.

For companies subject to GDPR or certification requirements, they keep an eye on data residency, access traceability and environment separation. These practices, often grouped under the DevSecOps banner, stop security from turning into a painful audit at the end of the project.

## How a DevOps mission unfolds

A DevOps mission moves forward in stages, each one bringing a visible win for your teams:

### Assessment

The DevOps engineer maps the infrastructure, pipelines, deployment practices and costs. They pinpoint what frustrates your developers and the biggest risks, then propose a prioritised roadmap.

### Foundations

They put the essential building blocks in place: infrastructure as code, a reliable deployment pipeline, secrets management and baseline monitoring. Every change goes through review and can be rolled back.

### Automation and reliability

Deployments become routine: ephemeral environments, progressive rollouts, automatic rollback, well-tuned alerts. Delivery metrics (deployment frequency, lead time to production, change failure rate) act as the compass.

### Handover

The DevOps engineer documents the architecture, writes the runbooks and trains your team so it stays autonomous once the mission is over.

## DevOps engineer, back-end developer or data engineer?

A [**back-end developer**](/en/roles/freelance-back-end-developer) builds the application logic and APIs; the DevOps engineer makes sure that code is built, deployed and operated properly. They work hand in hand, and many back-end developers have a DevOps mindset without making it their core craft.

A [**data engineer**](/en/roles/freelance-data-engineer) builds data pipelines, often with the same tools (Terraform, containers, orchestration) but in the service of data rather than the application. To shape your technical foundations as a whole, a [**tech lead**](/en/roles/freelance-tech-lead) can round out the setup.
