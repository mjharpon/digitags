A freelance growth marketer drives your product's growth through experimentation. They analyse every step of the journey, from acquisition to retention, find the most promising levers and test them quickly, keeping only what works.

Sitting where marketing, data and product meet, they aren't tied to a single channel: they work on activation, conversion, retention and referral, with a rigorous method and measured results.

## What a freelance growth marketer does

A growth marketer starts with a simple question: where is the biggest bottleneck to growth? They model the full funnel, often using the AARRR framework (acquisition, activation, retention, revenue, referral), spot where users drop off and focus effort where the impact will be greatest.

They form hypotheses, prioritise them, run experiments and learn from the results. A new sign-up page, an onboarding email sequence, a reworked trial offer or a referral programme: every initiative is measured before it's rolled out.

Their work cuts across teams. They partner with product on onboarding, with developers on tracking, with the [**data analyst**](/en/roles/freelance-data-analyst) on cohort analysis, and with paid media experts on acquisition.

## Typical growth marketing missions

Depending on your stage, a growth marketer can take on:

- **Funnel audit**: map the journey, set the right metrics and identify the steps holding growth back.
- **Experimentation programme**: build a hypothesis backlog, prioritise with frameworks such as ICE and run rigorous A/B tests.
- **Activation and onboarding**: shorten time to first value, working with the product team on a SaaS product or an app.
- **Lifecycle and CRM**: design email, push and in-app sequences with Brevo, Customer.io, HubSpot or Braze, segmented by behaviour.
- **Product-led growth**: shape the freemium or trial offer, viral loops, referral and the usage signals that come before conversion.

## Growth marketer tools and skills

A growth marketer relies on a solid analytics stack: GA4 for traffic, Amplitude, Mixpanel or PostHog for product analytics, Looker Studio or Metabase for dashboards. They can write a tracking plan, check data quality and read a cohort analysis.

For experimentation, they use tools such as AB Tasty, Kameleoon, Optimizely or the feature flags built into a product platform, and they know the statistical basics: sample size, test duration, biases to avoid. They're also fluent in no-code and automation tools (Webflow, Zapier, Make) to test fast without tying up the whole engineering team.

Finally, they build GDPR in from the start: cookie consent, privacy-friendly measurement, email list management and unsubscribes.

## How a growth marketing mission unfolds

A growth mission follows a continuous improvement loop:

### Diagnosis

The growth marketer analyses your data, your funnel and your current channels. They check the tracking is reliable and define the key metric to move — the North Star Metric.

### Hypothesis backlog

Building on the diagnosis, they draw up a list of experiment ideas, each framed as a measurable hypothesis, and prioritise them by expected impact, confidence and effort.

### Experiment cycles

Tests launch at a steady cadence, are analysed rigorously and documented. Winners are rolled out; losers bring lessons that are just as valuable.

### Scaling what works

The levers that work are automated and built into the team's processes. The growth marketer hands over their method, dashboards and library of learnings.
